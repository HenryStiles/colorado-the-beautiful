# Colorado the Beautiful: Celebrating 150 Years of Wild Places

## Page Title: Colorado the Beautiful
## Subtitle: Celebrating 150 years of the Centennial State's natural wonders and the wild places we must protect.

### Introduction: The Centennial State at 150
On August 1, 2026, Colorado celebrates its 150th birthday. For a century and a half, our state has been defined by its breathtaking natural beauty—from the towering, snow-capped peaks of the Rocky Mountains and the rugged red rock formations of the West, to the lifegiving waters of the Dolores and Yampa rivers, and the wild prairies where bison and pronghorn roam. 

To mark this historic sesquicentennial milestone, Environment Colorado has curated **Colorado the Beautiful**, an interactive photo gallery showcasing 149 stunning images submitted by Coloradans, legislators, photographers, and nature lovers from every corner of the state.

These photographs capture more than just scenery; they capture our shared heritage. Every image tells a story of a place that brings us peace, adventure, and connection—and serves as a powerful reminder of why we must protect these precious landscapes for the next 150 years.

---

### Why We Must Protect Colorado's Natural Heritage
Colorado’s wild places face unprecedented challenges. Rapid development, rising temperatures, and plastic pollution threaten the delicate ecosystems that wildlife—like the pika, bighorn sheep, and elk—call home. Many of the spectacular vistas featured in our gallery are vulnerable to mining, drilling, and commercial exploitation.

To preserve Colorado the Beautiful, we must commit to environmental stewardship:
*   **Protecting Public Lands:** We must keep our public lands public and work to designate new wilderness areas, ensuring habitats remain connected and undisturbed.
*   **Conserving Our Waterways:** From local mountain creeks to major rivers, keeping our water free from toxic runoff and plastic litter is vital for wildlife and clean drinking water.
*   **Reducing Plastic Pollution:** By reducing our reliance on single-use plastics, we can prevent trash from clogging our parks, trails, and rivers.
